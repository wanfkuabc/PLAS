\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{MLLM-based referring image segmentation.}
Recent advances in multimodal large language models
(MLLMs) have enabled powerful visual-language
reasoning for referring image segmentation (RIS).
LISA~\cite{Lai2024LISA} and
GSVA~\cite{Xia2024GSVA} employ segmentation-token
embeddings to guide SAM-based mask decoding, while
GLaMM~\cite{Rasheed2024GLaMM} extends this approach
to pixel-level grounding with an image-conditioned
decoder. PixelLM~\cite{Ren2024PixelLM} introduces
a multi-scale segmentation codebook and a lightweight
pixel decoder to integrate segmentation-token
embeddings with visual features.
More recent methods explore alternative mask
representation mechanisms.
HiMTok~\cite{Wang2025HiMTok} learns hierarchical
mask tokens for coarse-to-fine mask representation,
while PSALM~\cite{Zhang2024PSALM} integrates mask
tokens and a mask decoder within a unified
segmentation framework.
Other approaches, including
SAM4MLLM~\cite{Chen2024SAM4MLLM},
Seg-Zero~\cite{Liu2025SegZero},
SegAgent~\cite{Zhu2025SegAgent}, and
Text4Seg~\cite{Lan2025Text4Seg},
formulate localization or segmentation through
generated coordinates, reasoning sequences,
or patch-level predictions.
Although effective, these approaches employ
different output interfaces to connect multimodal
representations with pixel-level segmentation.
In contrast, PLAS focuses on extracting spatially
organized segmentation features directly from
MLLM hidden states through learnable multimodal
readout. By combining multi-layer feature fusion
with explicit teacher-guided mask-latent alignment,
PLAS transfers mask-level geometric knowledge
to the parallel readout representations before
image-conditioned SAM decoding.

\noindent\textbf{Parallel prediction and multimodal
	feature readout.}
Recent methods have also explored parallel
segmentation prediction to avoid autoregressive
coordinate or patch generation.
STAMP~\cite{Liu2026STAMP} simultaneously predicts
mask units within a single forward pass,
but its output remains a set of discrete patch
decisions.
However, parallel mask prediction differs
from parallel multimodal feature readout:
the former directly predicts segmentation
outputs, whereas the latter focuses on
extracting task-relevant representations
from multimodal hidden states.
PLAS adopts a learnable spatial readout
mechanism that exploits image--text interactions
within the MLLM and combines multi-layer
hidden states with spatial refinement.
This enables parallel extraction of
segmentation-aware features without
autoregressive mask-token generation.

\noindent\textbf{Geometry-supervised alignment
	and mask decoding.}
Despite their strong semantic understanding,
MLLM representations are not inherently
optimized for pixel-level geometric prediction.
Existing approaches typically bridge multimodal
features and segmentation through feature
projection, mask prompts, or external decoders
\cite{Lai2024LISA,Rasheed2024GLaMM,
Chen2024SAM4MLLM,Zou2023XDecoder}.
SAM~\cite{Kirillov2023SAM} provides a powerful
image-conditioned decoding framework that
generates masks from sparse or dense prompts.
However, optimizing segmentation primarily
through final mask losses provides limited
explicit supervision for the geometric
structure of intermediate multimodal features.
In contrast, PLAS constructs geometry-aware
mask latents from ground-truth masks through
dual reconstruction supervision.
A progressive teacher--student framework
then explicitly aligns spatially adapted
parallel readout features with these
teacher-derived mask latents before
image-conditioned SAM decoding.
This establishes a direct supervisory bridge
between multimodal feature extraction
and pixel-level mask geometry.
